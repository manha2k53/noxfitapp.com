Pilates for Weight Loss: Does It Actually Work?

By NoxFit Team  ·  7 min read  ·  Jul 2026

Pilates has an image problem when it comes to weight loss — it's often seen as too gentle to burn meaningful calories. That reputation is partly deserved and partly wrong. The honest picture: Pilates alone is unlikely to drive significant fat loss, but it has real benefits that make it a useful component of a broader weight loss programme — especially for people who can't do high-impact exercise.

Does Pilates Actually Help With Weight Loss?

Pilates can contribute to weight loss, but it's not primarily a calorie-burning exercise. Its main mechanism for weight loss is indirect: building lean muscle (particularly core and stabiliser muscles), improving body awareness and posture, and reducing injury risk that might otherwise prevent people from doing higher-intensity exercise.

A 2021 study in PLOS ONE found that 8 weeks of Pilates training in overweight women produced significant reductions in BMI, waist circumference, and hip circumference compared to a control group — without any dietary changes. The effects were meaningful but modest. More importantly, the study found significant improvements in body composition (muscle-to-fat ratio) even when total weight loss was modest.

The honest verdict: If you enjoy Pilates and do it consistently, it will contribute to weight loss — particularly body recomposition (losing fat, gaining muscle). But for those primarily focused on maximising fat loss per hour, higher-intensity alternatives like HIIT, cycling, or strength training will outperform it for calorie burn. The best approach: use Pilates strategically.

Compared to running (~550–700 kcal/hr) or cycling (~400–600 kcal/hr), basic mat Pilates burns significantly fewer calories per hour. However, Reformer Pilates at vigorous intensity approaches moderate cardio territory — and the muscle-building stimulus it provides continues burning calories post-session.

Best Pilates Styles for Fat Loss

Reformer Pilates

The highest calorie burn of any Pilates format. The spring-resistance system of the Reformer adds genuine muscular load across the entire body, engaging large muscle groups (legs, back, glutes) that mat Pilates can't load as effectively. Vigorous Reformer classes approach the calorie burn of moderate jogging. The downside: requires equipment or studio access, typically £15–30 per class.

Power Pilates / HIIT Pilates

Modern hybrid classes that incorporate Pilates principles with cardio intervals — jumping, fast-tempo sequences, weighted movements. These burn 300–450 kcal/hr and are significantly more effective for weight loss than traditional mat Pilates. Available on most fitness apps and YouTube.

Traditional Mat Pilates

The lowest calorie burn but the most accessible — requires no equipment, can be done anywhere, and is excellent for recovery days, improving flexibility, and building the core strength that makes other exercises more effective. Best used as a complement to higher-intensity training, not as the sole fat loss strategy.

Both are roughly equivalent for direct calorie burn. Yoga has a stronger evidence base for stress reduction and cortisol management — which matters if stress is driving your weight gain. Pilates has a stronger evidence base for core strength and postural improvement. For weight loss specifically, both work best as supplements to a higher-intensity programme rather than standalone approaches.

How to Combine Pilates With Other Exercise

The most effective structure for someone who enjoys Pilates but wants meaningful fat loss:

  • 2–3× strength training or HIIT for primary calorie burn and muscle building
  • 1–2× Pilates as active recovery — it promotes blood flow and mobility without adding significant fatigue
  • Daily walking (8,000–10,000 steps) to maximise total daily calorie expenditure without recovery cost

Pilates on rest days between harder sessions is particularly effective — it keeps you active without compromising recovery for the sessions that matter most for fat loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many times a week should I do Pilates to lose weight?
If Pilates is your primary exercise, 4–5 sessions per week is needed to create meaningful calorie burn — though even this will produce modest results compared to higher-intensity alternatives. If you're combining Pilates with cardio or strength training, 2–3 sessions per week as a complement is ideal. The most important factor is consistency: 3 Pilates sessions per week done consistently for months will outperform 5 sessions per week done sporadically.
Can Pilates flatten your stomach?
Pilates is excellent for strengthening the deep abdominal muscles (transverse abdominis), which can create a more toned, pulled-in appearance at the waist — even before significant fat is lost. However, "flattening" the stomach in terms of reducing belly fat requires overall calorie deficit and body fat reduction. Pilates helps by building the underlying muscle structure and improving posture, which makes the midsection look more defined as fat reduces.
Is Pilates good for beginners who are overweight?
Yes — it's one of the best starting points for overweight beginners precisely because it's zero-impact and highly modifiable. There's no running, jumping, or heavy loading on joints. Many overweight individuals find high-impact exercise painful or discouraging at first; Pilates provides a way to build strength, body awareness, and exercise habit without those barriers. As fitness improves, adding higher-intensity exercise becomes easier and safer.
