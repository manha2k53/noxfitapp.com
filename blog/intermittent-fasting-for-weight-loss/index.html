How Intermittent Fasting Causes Weight Loss

Intermittent fasting (IF) is an eating pattern that cycles between periods of fasting and eating. It doesn't specify which foods to eat — only when to eat them.

Weight loss from IF comes from two main mechanisms:

1. Calorie reduction by default

When you restrict your eating to a shorter window, most people naturally eat fewer calories — not because they're deliberately restricting, but because there's less time to eat. Research consistently shows IF produces calorie deficits of 300–500 calories per day without conscious calorie counting, which explains much of its effectiveness.

2. Hormonal changes that favour fat burning

During fasting periods, insulin levels drop significantly. Since insulin is the primary hormone that signals the body to store fat, lower insulin creates conditions where fat burning increases. After 12–16 hours of fasting, the body begins shifting more energy production toward fat oxidation. Growth hormone also rises during extended fasting, which helps preserve muscle mass while losing fat.

The honest truth: Research comparing IF to standard calorie restriction with equal calories consistently shows similar weight loss. IF works mainly because it helps people eat less — the hormonal effects are real but secondary. That said, many people find IF easier to sustain than daily calorie counting, which makes it genuinely useful.

For most people, 16/8 is the best starting point — effective, sustainable, and easy to fit around a normal schedule.

How to Do 16/8 (The Most Popular Method)

16/8 means fasting for 16 hours and eating within an 8-hour window each day. The simplest version: stop eating after dinner at 8pm, skip breakfast, and eat your first meal at noon. Your eating window is 12pm–8pm.

Sample schedule

TimeWhat Happens
8:00 PMLast meal of the day — eating window closes
8:00 PM – 12:00 PMFasting window (16 hours) — water, black coffee, plain tea OK
12:00 PMEating window opens — first meal
12:00 PM – 8:00 PMNormal eating — 2–3 meals

What breaks a fast?

Calories break a fast — even small ones. These are safe during the fasting window: water, black coffee (no milk or sugar), plain herbal tea, sparkling water. Avoid: bulletproof coffee (has calories), diet sodas (debated but best avoided), any food or caloric drink.

Adjusting the window

The 12–8pm window is the most popular but not mandatory. If you train in the morning, shift to 10am–6pm or 9am–5pm. If you prefer eating dinner late, try 2pm–10pm. The specific hours don't matter — what matters is keeping the fast at 16 hours consistently.

What to Eat in Your Eating Window

IF doesn't prescribe specific foods, but what you eat still determines results. Eating junk food in an 8-hour window won't produce weight loss — the calorie deficit still needs to be there.

Priorities for the eating window:

  • Protein first: 30–40g per meal to preserve muscle and stay full. Eggs, chicken, fish, Greek yogurt, legumes.
  • Vegetables at every meal: High volume, low calories, high satiety.
  • Limit ultra-processed foods: They're engineered to be easy to overeat and will undo the calorie deficit IF creates.
  • Don't try to compensate for the fast: Many beginners overeat in the window because they feel they "earned" it. You haven't — the goal is a deficit, not a feast.

Common Mistakes

Overeating in the eating window

The most common reason IF doesn't work. If you eat 2,500 calories in your 8-hour window when your maintenance is 2,000, you'll gain weight regardless of when you eat. IF helps reduce appetite — don't override it.

Quitting after 1–2 weeks

Hunger during the fasting window is real for the first 1–2 weeks as your body adapts. Most people find it significantly easier by week 3. Quitting during the adaptation phase means you never experience the full benefit.

Doing too much too fast

Starting with OMAD or 20/4 when you've never fasted is unnecessary and unsustainable. Start with 12/12 for a week, then 14/10, then 16/8. Gradual progression is more effective.

Not drinking enough water

Hunger and thirst feel identical. During the fasting window, drinking 500ml of water when you feel hungry eliminates a significant portion of perceived hunger.

FAQ

Can I exercise while fasting?

Yes. Many people train fasted with no performance issues, particularly for low-to-moderate intensity exercise. For high-intensity training or strength sessions, some people prefer to eat beforehand — experiment and see what works for you.

Will I lose muscle on IF?

Not if you eat enough protein and do resistance training. IF combined with adequate protein intake and strength training preserves muscle well during fat loss.

How long until I see results?

Most people notice scale changes within 1–2 weeks. Visible body composition changes typically take 4–8 weeks of consistent IF with a calorie deficit.

Is IF safe for everyone?

IF is not appropriate for people who are pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, or have a history of disordered eating. People with diabetes or on medication should consult a doctor before starting.
