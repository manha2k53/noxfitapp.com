Running vs Walking for Weight Loss: Which Burns More Fat?

Running burns roughly twice as many calories per minute as walking at the same effort level. But that's not the full picture.

Where Running Wins

More calories per minute

Running at 10 km/h burns ~340 calories in 30 minutes. Walking briskly burns ~160. If you have 30 minutes and want to maximise calorie burn, running wins every time.

Greater EPOC afterburn

Running — especially at higher intensities — creates a larger EPOC (excess post-exercise oxygen consumption) effect. Your metabolism stays elevated for 1–4 hours after a run, burning an extra 50–100 calories. Walking produces minimal EPOC.

Cardiovascular fitness

Running improves VO2 max and cardiovascular fitness significantly faster than walking. Better cardiovascular fitness correlates with higher daily calorie burn, better insulin sensitivity, and long-term weight maintenance.

Where Walking Wins

Sustainability and injury risk

Running injury rates are high — studies estimate 40–80% of regular runners sustain an injury in any given year. Walking injury rates are dramatically lower. For weight loss over months and years, the exercise you can sustain without getting injured is more valuable than the one that burns more calories but keeps sidelining you.

Daily volume adds up

Most people can walk for 60–90 minutes comfortably. Few can run that long at any meaningful intensity. Walking 10,000 steps (~8km) burns 400–500 calories — comparable to a 30-minute run — and most people can do it every single day.

Appetite effect

High-intensity exercise (including running) can increase appetite, partially offsetting the calorie burn. Walking at moderate intensity doesn't produce the same appetite spike, making it easier to maintain a calorie deficit after the session.

Accessible for beginners

Walking requires no fitness base, no equipment, and no technique. For people who are overweight, have joint issues, or are returning after a break, walking is the right starting point — not a consolation prize.

Same Distance: Who Burns More?

Interestingly, running and walking the same distance burn similar total calories — roughly 60–80 calories per km for a 75kg person, regardless of speed. Running gets you there faster; walking takes longer. The difference is mainly time efficiency, not metabolic magic.

Example: Walking 5km burns ~350 calories and takes ~45 minutes. Running 5km burns ~370 calories and takes ~28 minutes. Nearly identical total burn — the run just gets it done in less time.

The Verdict

Running burns more calories per unit of time. Walking is more sustainable, has lower injury risk, and is easier to do daily in higher volumes. Neither is categorically "better" — the best choice depends entirely on your starting fitness, injury history, and what you'll actually stick to.

For pure calorie burn efficiency: run. For long-term, injury-free fat loss: walk more (especially at incline). For the best results: combine both.
